THE DRAFT PRIMER

Winning in Style

Choose your cards. Build a deck. Play.

Stuck on a pick?

Need another color? Look for fixing. Missing early plays? Look for something cheaper. Can't handle their threats? Take an answer. Otherwise, choose a card that helps your plan.

Fixing gives you permission to play more colors.

A great spell doesn't help much if it stays in your hand. Take the lands and fixing that let you cast the cards you want to play.

Count your mana sources

Need a color early? About 9 sources is a starting point. Need it later? Around 6. One late splash card? Around 3.

These are rough guidelines, not guarantees. Multiple colored symbols and early spells need stronger support.

A dual land can supply either of its colors. A fetch only helps if your deck has a land it can find.

Mana creatures and Treasure makers are conditional: you must cast or create them first. If green casts your fixing, support green before adding other colors.

Look for several plays at two and three mana. They let you join the game early instead of watching your opponent build a board.

A cheap creature or removal spell can help now and still leave mana for another spell later. A combat trick needs a creature and the right moment, so don't count it as your whole early game.

04 · INTERACT

Answer their best card.

Illustration

Take a few cheap answers, not just threats. Removal can stop their best creature or clear a blocker so you can attack.

Destroy, exile, bounce, and counterspells all help, but they don't do the same job. Bounce buys time; a counterspell must catch the spell before it resolves.

See interaction
05 · FINISH

Have a way through.

Illustration

Have a few ways to keep making progress when the ground fills up. Flying, menace, trample, unblockable attackers, or removal can help you get through.

They can be cards you already wanted to play. You don't need a separate pile of expensive finishers.

See a way through
06 · PLAN

Draft a deck, not a pile.

Illustration

Think about how your cards work together. Are you attacking early, building toward a big turn, or answering threats until you can take over?

You don't need a perfect combo. A deck with reliable mana and useful early plays can work well without much synergy.

Start with 17 lands + 23 nonlands.

Lands let you cast your spells; nonlands give you things to do. Staying at 40 cards helps you draw your best cards more often. Add basic lands after drafting.

Adjust for your curve and game plan, not one unlucky opening hand. There isn't one creature count that fits every deck.

READY

Before game one.

Unused cards become your sideboard.

Want some numbers to start with?

Start with 17 lands and 23 nonlands. Within those nonlands, an example to check against is five two-mana plays, four three-mana plays, four answers, and three ways through a stall.

These are example counts, not quotas or a proven best list. Roles overlap: a cheap removal spell can be an early play and an answer. A flying creature can be an early play and a way through.

Frank Karsten's Arena Cube midrange example uses about five two-drops and five three-drops. His aggressive example starts much lower. Borrow the idea of a busy early curve, not a fixed creature recipe.

Why several of each? You won't draw every card. One early creature or one answer won't reliably show up when you need it. Check your pile between packs and fill the gaps.

Mana is a separate check: roughly 9 sources for a main color, 6 for a later secondary color, or 3 for one late splash are starting guidelines, not guarantees. Early double-colored costs need more. See the mana explanation before adding another color.

Advice worth keeping

LSV, on Vintage Cube: "There is no shortage of powerful cards". His point: choose cards that work toward the deck you are building, rather than collecting impressive cards with no shared plan.

Caleb Durward: "When in doubt on a pick, hedging towards the cheaper card". Check what your deck is missing before your next pick: early plays, interaction, or fixing.

Reid Duke: "There's no 'master formula' for how many cards of each mana cost you should play." Your plan and the speed of the games matter. The example counts above are a place to begin, not a test you have to pass.
